# Literature Review
**1. Problem Context**

This project focuses on helping learners understand the relationship between random mutation and non-random selection in evolutionary processes.

Needfinding revealed that although students can understand simple causal relationships (e.g., temperature affects survival), they often struggle with abstract probabilistic reasoning, especially when interpreting evolution as a filtering process rather than a goal-directed mechanism.

In particular, students tend to:

Misinterpret evolution as intentional or goal-driven
Fail to understand how traits interact with changing environments
Lack opportunities to observe **long-term evolutionary dynamics

To address this, our project proposes an AI-driven interactive simulation, where learners experience rapid evolutionary cycles and receive real-time feedback to understand survival dynamics under environmental pressure.

Research on intelligent tutoring systems shows that AI can provide adaptive feedback and personalized guidance during learning (Anderson et al., 1995).
These systems monitor learner behavior and provide step-by-step scaffolding, helping students build conceptual understanding.
Relevance:
This supports our idea of an AI agent as a “Scientific Logic Interpreter”, offering explanations rather than just outcomes.

Recent work on large language models (e.g., ChatGPT) shows that AI can generate natural language explanations and contextual feedback (Kasneci et al., 2023).

Provide reasoning-based feedback rather than just answers

**3. Key Ideas from the Literature**

Across these sources, several technological approaches emerge.
First, natural language processing and feedback generation: Many studies use natural language processing techniques to generate learning feedback. These techniques not only focus on simple grammar and syntax errors but also identify misconceptions in students' understanding. For example, feedback generated by large language models can help students understand causal relationships in the learning process through natural language explanations, rather than just providing answers（Kasneci et al., 2023）.

Second, gamification and simulation systems have been shown to have great potential in biology and evolutionary education, helping students deepen their understanding of abstract concepts through rapid feedback loops（Gee, 2003）. These systems can simulate ecological and evolutionary processes, allowing students to experiment and reflect, thereby enhancing their understanding of complex ecological and biological mechanisms.

Third, intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) have been applied across various disciplines to provide personalized learning paths and feedback（Anderson et al., 1995）. Through this personalized tutoring, students can receive more precise, progress-related feedback, which enhances learning outcomes. In evolutionary education, this approach can help students understand the role of mechanisms like random mutation and natural selection under different environmental pressures.

Last but not least, AI not only supports the personalization and feedback of the simulation process but also provides real-time causal explanations through the embedding of evolutionary-ecological trade-off simulations. This approach fills the gap in current systems that lack causal reasoning and mechanism feedback in dynamic ecological environments.

**4. Existing Solutions and Their Limitations**

Although some progress has been made by other scholars in the field of evolutionary education, existing solutions in academia still have the following limitations:

First, there is a limitation in feedback. Many existing automated writing evaluation tools focus on surface-level language features such as grammar and syntax, neglecting deeper aspects of argumentation and conceptual understanding. This is similarly applicable to existing simulation and learning tools in evolutionary education. Although they can simulate ecological processes, they often lack in-depth explanations of evolutionary mechanisms and causal feedback (Wilensky & Rand, 2015).

Second, many tools provide scores or brief comments but lack sufficient explanations, making it difficult for students to understand how to improve their work. Similarly, although existing AI simulation systems can provide feedback, they often lack causal explanations and mechanisms for the decisions students make, making it hard for them to understand why some traits are beneficial for survival while others fail (Kasneci et al., 2023).

Furthermore, high configuration barriers are an issue that cannot be ignored. Existing simulation tools in evolutionary education may require complex system setups and parameter adjustments, which affects their widespread adoption.

Finally, the most challenging issue is the reliability and bias of large language models. While large language models perform well in generating feedback, their reliability, bias, and pedagogical alignment in educational settings remain a challenge (Kasneci et al., 2023). Therefore, when applying these models to evolutionary education, it is essential to ensure that they can provide reliable and unbiased feedback.

**5. Implications for Your Project**

AI can effectively support automated learning feedback, but existing tools often struggle to balance the practicality of feedback with pedagogical clarity. The AI-driven feedback system in our project can fill this gap by providing real-time causal reasoning and mechanism explanations, rather than simply presenting results.

Although existing gamification and simulation learning systems can enhance student engagement, their feedback mechanisms often remain at a surface level. Our project aims to design more refined feedback loops that help students understand the interaction between "random mutation" and "non-random selection" during the simulation process, transforming these dynamic feedbacks into core learning content.

In the future, AI can serve as a "feedback interpreter" or "revision coach" rather than replacing teacher feedback. AI can transform brief comments into clear explanations and specific improvement suggestions, helping students better understand how to improve their understanding of evolution, thus reducing the workload of teachers.
